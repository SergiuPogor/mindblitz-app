Accessibility

Last updated: 2026-11-07 · Version 1.1

MEDIAPRONET SRL wants MindBlitz and its website to be usable by as many people as possible. This page explains what we do, what does not work well yet, and how to tell us about a problem.

The website

We aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA. We have checked the site ourselves; it has not been audited by an outside expert, so we do not claim full conformance. The website:

  • works with a keyboard, with a skip link and a visible focus outline
  • uses headings, landmarks and text descriptions for images, so that screen readers can follow it
  • has light and dark themes, follows your device's setting, and keeps text contrast high in both
  • lets you zoom to 200% and use narrow screens without scrolling sideways
  • respects the reduced motion setting of your device
  • loads no third-party scripts or fonts, so it stays fast on slow connections

Known limits on the website: the demo game is timed and asks you to judge colours, so it may not suit people who are colour blind or who need more time. It is optional, and nothing else on the site depends on it.

The app

The app follows Android's accessibility guidance where it can: buttons are large, text uses your phone's font size setting in most places, sound and vibration can be turned off, and nothing depends on sound alone.

Comfort settings in the app

Open the Me tab and find the Comfort card. Each option is saved on your device and applies across the whole app:

  • Colour-blind-safe colours. Right and wrong answers, and the rating colours, use blue and orange instead of green and red. A tick or a cross also appears after each answer, and rating tiles show a tick, a dash or a cross, so the meaning never depends on colour alone. The text you share uses blue, orange and black squares instead of green, yellow and red.
  • Larger text. Makes all text in the app 15% bigger, on top of your phone's own font size.
  • Reduced motion. Turns off screen shake, the drifting background and confetti (a gentle fade is shown instead), and replaces zooming screen changes with a simple fade. It also turns on by itself when Android's "Remove animations" setting is on.
  • Left-handed Duel. Mirrors the answer buttons in Duel.
  • Vibration. Off, light or strong.

Practice and tutorials

  • Practice mode. In free play, every game can be played as Practice: there is no timer, no lives are lost, and nothing changes your score, rating, XP or coins. After a mistake you see a gentle "Try again", and many games explain the right answer and wait until you tap Next. Tap End to stop at any time.
  • Tutorials. The first time you open a game, a short tutorial points at what to tap. You can skip it, and replay it with "Show tutorial" on the game's start screen.

Known limits in the app

  • Outside Practice, most games are timed and rely on quick visual reactions, so they are hard to play with a screen reader such as TalkBack. Menus and settings work better than the games themselves.
  • Some games ask you to tell colours or shades apart, such as Color Clash and Shade Spotter. Colour-blind-safe colours do not change the colours these games are about. Many other games do not use colour.
  • Some games flash or move quickly, even with reduced motion on. See Health and Safety.
  • The app works in portrait orientation only.

We keep improving these points with each update.

Tell us about a problem

If you find something hard to use, or need information in another form, email mediapronetsrl@gmail.com. Please tell us the page or screen, what you tried to do, and the device or assistive technology you use. We will try to help and to fix the problem.
